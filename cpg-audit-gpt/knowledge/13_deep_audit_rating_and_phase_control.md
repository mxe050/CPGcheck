# 13 Deep Audit Rating and Phase Control

## Purpose
Control what happens after whole-guideline triage. Prevent stopping at target selection. Define sentinel deep audit, methodological rating, and final CPG trustworthiness conclusion.

This file also prevents an important recurring error: rating down a CPG merely because details are not fully reproduced inside the uploaded file. The correct standard is practical verifiability and internal consistency across accessible materials, not complete recalculation from the uploaded file alone.

---

## Phase completion rule
Whole-guideline triage is preparation, not endpoint. Unless the user explicitly requests triage only, proceed in the same answer:
1. whole-guideline triage;
2. priority queue;
3. sentinel target selection;
4. sentinel deep audit;
5. evidence-to-recommendation traceability assessment;
6. sentinel methodological rating;
7. Final CPG trustworthiness conclusion.

Do not stop after “next deep audit target is ...”. Continue: “Sentinel target selected. Proceeding to sentinel deep audit.”

---

## Sentinel cross-check
Do not evaluate by CQ text alone. Cross-check, when available:
1. CQ text;
2. recommendation table;
3. SoF / Evidence Profile / evidence summary;
4. SR, NMA, or meta-analysis report;
5. search strategy;
6. explanation/rationale text;
7. EtD or equivalent;
8. COI and voting/consensus records.

If some details are outside the uploaded file but are clearly located in an appendix, supplement, linked SR/NMA paper, protocol, or public web page, treat those materials as part of the verifiable evidence chain. Do not restrict the audit to the uploaded file alone.

---

## Practical verifiability, not complete reproducibility
A rating should not be lowered merely because the auditor cannot completely recalculate every search, downgrade, NMA credibility judgment, or COI decision from the uploaded material.

Use this standard:

- **Practically verifiable**: the major judgment can be followed in the main text, appendix, supplement, linked SR/NMA paper, public web page, protocol, SoF, or Evidence Profile, and it is consistent with the recommendation.
- **Not practically verifiable**: even after considering accessible linked or referenced materials, the major judgment remains unclear, unsupported, inaccessible, or disconnected from the recommendation.
- **Internally inconsistent**: the recommendation, explanation text, SoF/SR/NMA/meta-analysis, certainty judgment, EtD, or COI handling contradict each other.

A-rating decisions must be based on practical verifiability and internal consistency, not on whether every detail is contained in the uploaded file.

Forbidden rating reasons:
- “not fully verifiable in the uploaded material, therefore not A”;
- “search/RoB/downgrade/NMA credibility cannot be fully recalculated, therefore not A”;
- “details are outside the main text, therefore not A”;
- “recommendation-level COI log is simplified, therefore not A.”

Acceptable rating reasons:
- “even including linked external materials, the key judgment is not practically verifiable”;
- “the recommendation conflicts with SoF/SR/NMA/EtD”;
- “the search does not capture the PICO”;
- “downgrade decisions and final certainty are inconsistent”;
- “important harms, costs, or patient values are ignored”;
- “relevant COI or industry influence exists but management is not traceable.”

---

## Internal consistency checks
Look for:
- explanation text saying high certainty while evidence table has downgrades;
- strong recommendation despite low/very low certainty without exception rationale;
- study-design counts used as evidence appraisal or recommendation justification;
- Minds/GRADE claims accepted without outcome-specific traceability;
- observational meta-analysis used without confounding and adjusted-effect checks;
- benefit emphasis while harms/QOL/burden are ignored;
- relative effect only or misleading absolute effect;
- NMA ranking used without uncertainty/credibility;
- vote/agreement replacing EtD;
- recommendation text contradicting SoF/SR/NMA/meta-analysis;
- relevant COI or industry influence without management.

---

## Certainty calculation checks
For GRADE/Minds/Core GRADE claims, do not accept final certainty at face value. Check:
- prespecified PICO and outcome-specific study selection;
- RCT starts high; observational evidence starts low;
- design-appropriate RoB, including non-randomized-study RoB when observational evidence is used;
- RoB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, and publication bias downgrades match final certainty;
- upgrade reasons are justified;
- SoF/Evidence Profile or equivalent is outcome-specific;
- EtD shows how benefits, harms, burden, values/preferences, resources,
  acceptability, and feasibility connect to recommendation direction and strength;
- multiple outcome certainty is summarized transparently;
- RCT, phase III trial, or statistical significance alone is not used as certainty.

Downgrade arithmetic mismatch is a major red flag. Low or very low certainty itself is not a rating penalty.

## Study-design count audit
For all clinical practice guidelines, reporting the number of RCTs,
observational studies, and case series is not a defect by itself. It becomes a
methodological problem only when the count is used as a substitute for
SR/GRADE/EtD appraisal.

During sentinel audit, check whether design counts are connected to:
- outcome-specific eligibility and study selection;
- design-appropriate RoB and confounding assessment;
- directness, precision, inconsistency, and publication bias judgments;
- SoF/Evidence Profile or equivalent;
- EtD explanation of how the evidence affects direction and strength.

If the recommendation relies on the count alone, or places RCTs,
observational studies, and case series on the same evidentiary level without
certainty logic, treat this as low substantive traceability even if the
literature search is described.

## Minds/GRADE claim audit
For all clinical practice guidelines, "Minds-compliant", "GRADE-based", or
"certainty was evaluated" is a claim requiring verification. It does not itself
raise the rating.

Treat the claim as partial/incomplete or failed when the sentinel evidence chain
lacks any major element needed for the claimed method: prespecified PICO,
outcome-specific selection, design-appropriate RoB, GRADE domain judgments,
outcome-specific SoF/Evidence Profile, EtD domains, certainty-strength
alignment, or transparent recommendation linkage.

Do not allow literature search, study-design counts, narrative rationale,
formal recommendation tables, voting, or agreement rate to replace GRADE/Minds
certainty assessment or EtD.

## Observational meta-analysis audit
For all clinical practice guidelines, observational meta-analysis must be
audited for confounding and appropriate effect estimates before it is treated
as trustworthy recommendation evidence.

Check:
- whether the question is confounding-sensitive;
- whether crude event counts, crude means, SDs, or unadjusted totals were
  pooled when adjusted estimates are needed;
- whether adjusted RR/OR/HR or equivalent estimates were used;
- whether different adjustment sets across studies are acknowledged;
- whether ROBINS-I or equivalent non-randomized-study RoB is used;
- whether observational estimates are separated from RCT evidence or combined
  with a transparent rationale;
- whether observational meta-analysis is overused to support strong
  recommendation or high certainty.

Crude observational meta-analysis used as recommendation justification is a
major red flag. An observational meta-analysis should not be downgraded
automatically when adjusted estimates, confounding, RoB, directness, precision,
and inconsistency are transparently handled.

---

## Search strategy audit
For high-risk sentinel targets, assess whether search terms capture PICO, controlled vocabulary/free text, synonyms, drug/device/class names, relevant abbreviations, time period, field tags, and separation from other CQs.

If the search strategy is outside the main text but accessible in an appendix, supplement, linked SR/NMA paper, or public protocol, do not penalize for main-text absence. Penalize only when the search is not practically verifiable or clearly fails to capture the PICO.

Use “SR claim partially supported / search strategy weak” when the search is traceable but materially fails to capture the PICO.

---

## Effect interpretation audit
Effects must be clinically interpretable. Check absolute effects, baseline risks, NNT/NNH, risk differences, mean differences, SMD interpretation, MID/thresholds, HR-to-absolute conversion when needed, and CI crossing benefit/no effect/harm. Statistical significance is not enough.

Do not penalize solely because QOL, harms, burden, or rare events were not quantitatively pooled. Penalize only if they were not searched, not considered, ignored in EtD, or presented in a way that misleads recommendation strength.

---

## NMA audit
For NMA-dependent recommendations, check network geometry, transitivity, incoherence, direct/indirect evidence, certainty per comparison, ranking uncertainty, and whether ranking was overused.

NMA credibility details may be in a linked NMA paper or supplement. Their location outside the uploaded file does not block A. A is blocked only if NMA credibility is not practically verifiable, internally inconsistent, or ranking is used as if it alone justified the recommendation.

---

## Strong recommendation gate
Strong recommendations require clear benefit-harm dominance, sufficiently certain evidence or explicit low-certainty exception, assessment of harms/burden/cost/feasibility, values/preferences including variability, and EtD rationale. High agreement is not enough.

Low or very low certainty with a conditional recommendation is not a rating penalty. Low or very low certainty with a strong recommendation is a red flag only when the exception rationale is not traceable.

---

## Consensus and voting
Voting, Delphi, and agreement rates are transparency records, not recommendation-validity evidence. If EtD is inconsistent, high agreement may suggest insufficient challenge rather than higher trust.

Do not penalize for reporting agreement rates. Penalize only when voting or agreement replaces evidence-to-decision reasoning.

---

## A-rating guardrails

### Do not block A for:
- very low certainty;
- conditional recommendation;
- recommendation not possible;
- QOL/harms not pooled when explained;
- acceptability/equity evidence being empirically limited but considered;
- external appendix, supplement, linked SR/NMA paper, protocol, or web reference;
- lack of complete recalculation;
- simplified COI log when no relevant COI, no industry funding, and no suspicious involvement are present.

### Block or lower A for:
- important judgment not practically verifiable even after accessible linked materials;
- source materials internally inconsistent;
- study-design counts used as a substitute for SR/GRADE/EtD appraisal;
- formal Minds/GRADE/SR/meta-analysis claims not supported by outcome-specific traceability;
- crude observational meta-analysis used as recommendation justification for a confounding-sensitive question;
- low/very low certainty with strong recommendation and no exception rationale;
- search strategy clearly missing the PICO;
- downgrade/final certainty inconsistency;
- recommendation contradicts SoF/SR/NMA/EtD;
- important harms, costs, burden, or values ignored;
- NMA ranking misuse;
- relevant COI, industry funding, or industry influence with unclear management.

---

## A/B rating reason filter
Before finalizing A/B/B−/C reasoning, inspect the explanation.

If it says or implies any of the following, revise it:
- “uploaded material does not allow full verification, therefore not A”;
- “search/RoB/downgrade/NMA details are not fully recalculable, therefore not A”;
- “main text lacks details, therefore not A”;
- “recommendation-level COI log is not detailed, therefore not A.”

Replace with one of the following only if supported:
- “even including linked external materials, the key judgment is not practically verifiable”;
- “the recommendation conflicts with SoF/SR/NMA/EtD”;
- “the search does not capture the PICO”;
- “downgrade and final certainty are inconsistent”;
- “important harms/costs/values are not reflected in EtD”;
- “relevant COI exists but management is not traceable.”

---

## Rating rubric
A: Recommendation-level PICO, SR/NMA or appropriate GRADE-ADOLOPMENT, search, RoB, certainty process, clinical effect interpretation, harms, values/preferences, resources, EtD domains, and COI management are practically verifiable and internally consistent. Evidence may be very low if handled transparently. Complete recalculation is not required.

B: Major recommendations are mostly traceable and internally coherent, but some important judgments remain only partially practically verifiable even after considering accessible appendices, supplements, linked SR/NMA papers, protocols, or web pages, or there are minor internal inconsistencies that are unlikely to change the recommendation.

B−: Methods are broadly present, but multiple important judgments are difficult to practically verify even after accessible linked materials, or there are important gaps or inconsistencies that could affect recommendation trustworthiness.

B/C boundary: Some recommendations are trustworthy, but guideline-level recommendation formation is substantially opaque or heterogeneous; GRADE vs non-GRADE separation may be incomplete.

C+ or below: Consider when downgrade/final certainty is inconsistent, search misses PICO, recommendation conflicts with evidence materials, major harms/costs/values are not reflected, NMA ranking is misused, vote replaces EtD, or relevant COI management is not traceable in a high-risk recommendation.

Also consider C+ or below when design counts, name-only SR/Minds/GRADE claims,
or crude observational meta-analysis are used as the main bridge from evidence
to recommendation without outcome-specific appraisal and EtD linkage.

D/E: Use for major evidence-recommendation rupture, serious misrepresentation, unjustifiable recommendation, or severe COI/methodology failure.

Rating reasons must not be certainty level itself, conditional recommendation itself, lack of uploaded-file completeness, or lack of complete reproducibility itself. Valid reasons are practical verifiability across accessible materials, internal consistency, certainty-strength alignment, and transparent limitations.

---

## Final CPG conclusion
Final output must return to the whole CPG:
- Final CPG rating candidate;
- trustworthiness conclusion: trustworthy / conditionally trustworthy / important concerns / difficult to trust;
- SR/NMA claim judgment;
- GRADE/Minds/Core GRADE consistency;
- GRADE vs non-GRADE separation;
- EtD traceability;
- GRADE-ADOLOPMENT judgment if relevant;
- what can be trusted;
- what lowers trust;
- sentinel implication;
- COI judgment;
- practical interpretation;
- residual uncertainty.
