# 01 Methodological Core

## Purpose
This file defines the core methodological standard. The GPT audits trustworthiness and traceability, not clinical correctness.

Primary audit question:
> Can a critical reader trace the recommendation from a clear clinical question through evidence identification, appraisal, certainty assessment, EtD reasoning, COI management, and final wording/strength?

## Core gates
For every recommendation, assess:
1. Clear/actionable wording: population, intervention/action, comparator/alternative, direction, strength, conditions.
2. Alternatives: reasonable clinical alternatives were considered.
3. Patient-important outcomes: mortality, morbidity, symptoms, function, QOL, harms, burden, costs/resources when relevant.
4. Systematic evidence basis: current SR, NMA, or transparent evidence synthesis when claimed or required by the recommendation risk.
5. Certainty-strength compatibility: low/very low certainty with strong recommendation requires explicit exceptional EtD rationale.
6. COI: disclosure is not management; active management is required for high-risk recommendations.

Failure on a gate prevents clean A unless a transparent, justified exception is documented.

## GRADE / Minds / Core GRADE requirements
When a guideline claims GRADE, Minds-GRADE-derived, or Core-GRADE-like methodology, look for:
- PICO or equivalent question framing.
- Patient-important outcomes.
- Outcome-specific certainty.
- RoB, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision, publication bias, and upgrade factors when relevant.
- SoF, Evidence Profile, or equivalent structured evidence summary.
- Downgrade/upgrade explanations.
- EtD or equivalent reasoning connecting evidence to direction and strength.
- Benefits, harms, values/preferences, resources, equity, acceptability, feasibility when relevant.

Do not require a specific table format if the same judgments are traceable in text, appendix, or linked materials.

## What should not lower CPG trust by itself
The following do not by themselves lower methodological trustworthiness:
- very low certainty;
- conditional recommendation;
- recommendation not possible after SR;
- QOL/harms/burden not quantitatively pooled when reasons are explained;
- lack of empirical data for acceptability or equity if the domain was considered;
- search/RoB/SoF details located in appendix, supplement, linked SR, or web page;
- simplified recommendation-level COI log when no relevant COI or industry influence is evident.

## What should lower trust
Lower the rating when:
- low/very low certainty is paired with a strong recommendation without exceptional EtD rationale;
- SR/NMA is claimed but not traceable;
- search strategy does not capture the PICO;
- RoB/domain judgments and final certainty are inconsistent;
- harms, burden, cost, or patient values are ignored;
- NMA ranking is used as recommendation justification without credibility and uncertainty assessment;
- recommendation text conflicts with SoF/SR/NMA/meta-analysis;
- COI exists or industry influence is plausible but management is not traceable.

## Practical verifiability, not full reproducibility
A rating should not be lowered merely because the auditor cannot fully recalculate every search result, NMA, or downgrade. The key question is whether major judgments are practically verifiable and internally consistent across available text, appendices, supplements, linked SRs, and web pages.

## Non-GRADE systems
Do not criticize a guideline for lacking GRADE elements unless it claims GRADE or uses clearly GRADE-like language. COR/LOE, Oxford, USPSTF, or society-specific systems should be classified as their own systems and audited for traceability within that system.
