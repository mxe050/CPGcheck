# 07 Claim-gated Audit Rule

Purpose: prevent overcalling non-GRADE or narrative guideline systems as GRADE/SR failure.

## GRADE claim status
Classify before auditing:
1. explicit GRADE claim: “GRADE was used,” GRADE approach, GRADE certainty, SoF/Evidence Profile/EtD explicitly named.
2. explicit non-GRADE system: COR/LOE, USPSTF, Oxford, society-specific evidence classification.
3. implicit or ambiguous GRADE-like claim: certainty/SoF/EtD-like language without explicit GRADE claim.
4. no GRADE claim found.

Rules:
- Use Unsupported GRADE claim only for explicit GRADE claim.
- For non-GRADE systems, write: non-GRADE evidence grading system; GRADE requirements not directly applicable.
- Do not treat non-GRADE use as low quality by itself.
- Still audit traceability, patient-important outcomes, harms, absolute effects, EtD-like reasoning, and COI management.

## SR/NMA claim status
Classify before auditing:
1. explicit SR/NMA claim.
2. evidence review claim.
3. narrative evidence summary only.
4. no SR/NMA claim found.

Rules:
- Use Unsupported SR/NMA claim only for explicit claim.
- For no SR claim, say SR claim absent / SR traceability not assessable in provided material.
- Do not call a narrative evidence summary an unsupported SR unless it claims SR.
- For high-risk recommendations, lack of SR traceability can still justify human-review priority.

## Scope discipline
Every “not found” must specify scope:
- not found in provided excerpt;
- not found in main text;
- appendix not checked;
- not found after checking supplement;
- publicly unverifiable.

Forbidden:
- expanding excerpt-level not found into full-guideline absence;
- calling GRADE/SR fraud without explicit claim;
- criticizing non-GRADE systems merely for not being GRADE.
